About the long way around

I came to design the long way around.

My bachelor's was in Electronics and Instrumentation - the closest I got to interfaces back then was wiring circuits. Then i drifted into graphic design somewhere along the way, and from there into frontend engineering & product design.
Four years in, I'm still here. I work at LeadSquared at Bengaluru, designing for SIERA - reporting and analytics tool, the platform-wide forms system along with LeadSquared's flagship AI product Pulse.

My inspirations and interests

The design problems I'm drawn to are the ones that don't fit on one screen. A query, an expression, a dashboard widget - none of these stand alone. One decision in one place has to hold up across a hundred others. That's the part of the work I find genuinely interesting: when you're not designing a thing, you're designing the rules that govern a hundred things.

The best interfaces I've used disappear. You don't notice them, because they're doing exactly what you need without asking for credit. That's the bar I work toward - interfaces that feel obvious in hindsight, even when the systems behind them aren't. It usually takes more decisions to get there, not fewer.

Looking ahead

The thing I want to keep getting better at is the thing I already do - solving problems through design. That means thinking faster, working more decisively, and getting closer to the engineering layer. Front-end and AI tooling are real parts of how products get built now, and I'd rather understand them deeply than design around them.

The other half of growth has nothing to do with pixels. It's learning to lead and inspire the people I work with - to communicate more clearly, listen more carefully, and make the work better for everyone in the room, not just the design file.
